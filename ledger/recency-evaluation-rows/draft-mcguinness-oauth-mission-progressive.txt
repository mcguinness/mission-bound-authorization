            Mission Progressive Authorization for OAuth 2.0
           draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission-progressive-latest

Abstract

   Mission Expansion for OAuth 2.0 widens an agent's authority only
   through a fresh human approval that creates a successor Mission.  An
   open-ended agentic task often cannot have its full authority
   enumerated at the initial approval, which leaves a deployment
   choosing between over-provisioning a broad standing Mission and
   interrupting the user for a fresh approval at every step.

   This document defines progressive authorization, an experimental
   third option.  At the initial approval, the Approver additionally
   consents to a bounded authority ceiling and a drawdown policy.  The
   Mission Issuer may then adjudicate an expansion that stays within
   that ceiling by policy rather than by a fresh human approval.
   Authority grows within the ceiling at runtime while the active
   authority any single Mission yields stays narrow, and each policy-
   adjudicated drawdown is bounded incrementally, never activating the
   whole ceiling at once.  Authority classes named by the runtime
   profile's high-consequence classification, and the external-
   communication exfiltration leg, always require a fresh human
   approval, even within the ceiling.

1.  Introduction

   Mission Expansion for OAuth 2.0
   [I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission-expansion] (the "expansion
   profile") defines the governed path from an authority shortfall to a
   new approval: a successor Mission, freshly consented, that supersedes
   its predecessor.  Every expansion under that profile is adjudicated
   by a fresh human approval.

   For a task whose growth is anticipated, that discipline has a human
   cost.  Each expansion is another approval moment, and an Approver
   asked too often stops reading what is asked (the consent-fatigue
   residual of [I-D.draft-mcguinness-mission-security-model]).  A
   deployment that avoids the prompts over-provisions a broad standing
   Mission instead.

   Progressive authorization is a third option.  The Approver consents
   once to a bounded envelope, the authority ceiling, and to a rule for
   drawing authority from it, the drawdown policy.  Authority then grows
   within the envelope at runtime without a fresh human approval each
   time, while the active authority any single Mission yields stays
   narrow.  The envelope can name a class of resources the agent will
   only meet during execution: the Approver consents once to the family,
   and every concrete binding stays inside it (Section 3.1.1).  One
   considered consent to a ceiling replaces many hurried consents to
   increments.

   This document is optional and experimental: adopt it for evaluation,
   not as a stable interface.  It removes the per-expansion human from a
   consented envelope, which is the highest-consequence capability in
   the expansion family.  This document therefore conditions the
   capability on the rate bounds, prohibited-class rules, and audit
   linkage it requires, and plain expansion remains the better fit where
   task authority can be anticipated per step.

   A Mission Issuer that does not implement this document adjudicates
   every expansion as a fresh human approval and is a fully conforming
   expansion-capable Mission Issuer
   ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission-expansion]).  Nothing here
   places a new requirement back on the expansion profile or the
   issuance profile.

1.2.  Conventions and Terminology

   The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL NOT",
   "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT", "RECOMMENDED", "NOT RECOMMENDED", "MAY", and

   This document uses Predecessor Mission, Successor Mission, and
   Expansion request as the expansion profile defines them.

   Authority ceiling:  The pre-consented maximum authority any expansion
      of a Mission may reach without a further human approval
      (Section 3).  This bound is distinct from the template profile's
      Template Ceiling on Missions dispatched from a template and from
      the shaping companion's caller-supplied shaping ceiling on a
      proposal ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission-template],
      [I-D.draft-mcguinness-mission-shaping]).

   Drawdown policy:  The policy under which the Mission Issuer may
      adjudicate an in-ceiling expansion by policy rather than by a
      fresh human approval (Section 3).

   Drawdown request:  An expansion request of a Mission that carries an
      authority ceiling, whether the Mission Issuer adjudicates it by
      policy or by a fresh human approval (Section 3.1).

   Policy-adjudicated drawdown:  A drawdown request that the Mission
      Issuer adjudicates under the drawdown policy and that commits a
      ceiling_drawdown successor (Section 3.1.2).

   Mission Deployment Profile:  The deployment-level manifest the
      architecture defines
      ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-mission-architecture]).  The bounds,
      mappings, and cadence this document requires a deployment to
      publish are published there.

2.  Protocol Overview

   A progressive Mission begins with an ordinary approval and grows
   through a chain of successor Missions:

   1.  At the initial approval, the Approver consents to the Mission, an
       authority ceiling, and a drawdown policy.  The Mission Issuer
       records the ceiling and the policy on the Mission and commits
       them with ceiling_hash (Section 3).

   2.  When the agent needs authority its Mission lacks, the client
       sends an expansion request: a token exchange presenting the
       Mission's access token
       ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission-expansion]).

   3.  A request outside the ceiling is never policy-adjudicated: it
       falls back to an ordinary, freshly approved expansion, subject to
       the Mission Issuer's policy (Section 3.1.1).

   4.  A request within the ceiling falls back to an ordinary, freshly
       approved expansion when it meets a fresh-approval condition
       (Section 3.1.3), when it exceeds a drawdown bound or would
       activate the entire remaining ceiling (Section 3.1.4), when the
       chain is past its ceiling review cadence (Section 3.2), or when
       the drawdown policy does not authorize it (Section 3.1.2).

   5.  Otherwise the drawdown policy adjudicates the request.  If the
       expansion profile's activation checks and any derivation limit
       admit it, the Mission Issuer creates the successor, supersedes
       the predecessor, and returns the successor's access token in the
       token-exchange response (Section 3.1.5).

   6.  A ceiling review, a fresh human approval that explicitly re-
       consents the ceiling (a narrowed one included), restarts the
       review cadence; approving a successor's authority alone does not
       (Section 3.2).

      Approver consents to Mission M0, ceiling C, drawdown policy P
                                   |
                                   v
         +------+  drawdown  +------+  drawdown  +------+
         |  M0  |----------->|  M1  |----------->|  M2  |---> ...
         +------+  under P   +------+  under P   +------+
        terminated          terminated            active
       (superseded)        (superseded)

                         Figure 1: A Drawdown Chain

   Every successor drawn under P stays within C, and each policy-
   adjudicated drawdown is bounded incrementally.

2.1.  Example Scenario

   The examples in this document follow one scenario, the ceiling-
   drawdown variant of the family's Q3 reconciliation baseline.  At
   2026-11-02T08:00:00Z, alice (user_3p2q8mN1a0kV7tR at
   https://idp.example.com) approves Mission
   msn_pasG9An7Ivj9cOdd3RqWWonuaLAbDHtM at https://as.example.com for
   the reconciliation agent, the client s6BhdRkqt3.  Its Authority Set
   allows only reading Q3 invoices at https://erp.example.com. alice
   also consents to an authority ceiling that adds posting journal
   entries of at most USD 500 and correcting Q3 invoices, under the
   drawdown policy q3-reconcile-drawdown version 1.

   The deployment's Mission Deployment Profile publishes a per-drawdown
   bound of one added action, a chain rate bound of two policy-
   adjudicated drawdowns per hour, and a 30-day ceiling review cadence.
   It maps invoices.read to the consequential read class, and journal-
   entries.write and invoices.write to the reversible consequential
   write class, so no ceiling action requires a fresh approval by class
   (Section 3.1.3).  The Mission has no Child Missions and its chain has
   no discharge or containment restrictions.

3.  Progressive Authorization

   At the initial approval event ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission]),
   the Approver MAY additionally consent to an authority ceiling and a
   drawdown policy, recorded as these Mission members:

   authority_ceiling:  An array of authorization-details-shaped entries,
      each the shape of an Authority Set entry
      ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission]).  It is the pre-consented
      maximum any expansion of this Mission may reach without a further
      human approval, and every in-ceiling successor MUST be within it
      (Section 3.1).

   drawdown_policy:  An activation policy reference, an object of id,
      version, and digest ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission],
      Section "Standing-Consent Bases").  It identifies the policy under
      which the Mission Issuer MAY adjudicate an in-ceiling expansion by
      policy rather than by a fresh human approval, and commits its
      content.  The policy's content is deployment-defined.

   Where present, authority_ceiling and drawdown_policy are recorded on
   the Mission and committed by a ceiling_hash.  Because ceiling_hash
   covers drawdown_policy, the ceiling commits the exact policy the
   human consented to, and the Mission Issuer verifies its digest before
   each policy-adjudicated drawdown under the issuance profile's
   activation policy commitment rule
   ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission], Section "Standing-Consent
   Bases").

   The ceiling_hash is computed with the issuance profile's integrity-
   anchor envelope ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission]):

   *  typ: mission-authority-ceiling;

   *  hashed object: a JSON object with exactly two members,
      authority_ceiling (the ceiling array) and drawdown_policy (the
      activation policy reference object), canonicalized as the
      integrity-anchor envelope requires, so member order follows that
      canonicalization;

   *  omission rule: when the Mission carries no drawdown_policy, the
      drawdown_policy member is omitted from the hashed object, never
      included as null or an empty object.

   ceiling_hash is an envelope anchor under the issuance profile's
   commitment mechanisms, which this document imports normatively.

   The Mission Issuer also records ceiling_consent_hash, the
   ceiling_hash of the ceiling and drawdown policy as the Approver
   consented them.  At a consent the two are equal.  A policy-
   adjudicated drawdown that narrows the ceiling recomputes ceiling_hash
   and leaves ceiling_consent_hash unchanged (Section 3.1.5); only an
   explicit re-consent replaces it (Section 3.1.6).

   Neither member is committed under authority_hash: authority_hash
   commits only the consented Authority Set
   ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission]), and the ceiling is a bound on
   future expansions, not present authority.

   At the initial approval, the consent disclosure MUST render the
   ceiling to the Approver, together with the fact that in-ceiling
   expansion is policy-adjudicated
   ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission-consent-evidence]).

   The approval surface establishes the consented ceiling and drawdown
   policy, which the Mission Issuer retains as Mission-record
   provenance.  This document defines no OAuth parameter or token claim
   that proposes a ceiling or reports the consented one.  A deployment's
   approval surface can take its own proposal, but client input alone
   never establishes the consent.

   A Mission that carries no authority_ceiling has no progressive
   authorization: every expansion of it is an ordinary, freshly approved
   expansion ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission-expansion]).

   The following is an example of the ceiling members the Mission Issuer
   records for the scenario's Mission at the initial approval
   (Section 2.1), other members omitted:

3.1.  In-Ceiling Expansion

   An *in-ceiling expansion* is an expansion, adjudicated per the
   expansion profile ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission-expansion]),
   whose successor Authority Set is within the predecessor's consented
   authority_ceiling.

3.1.1.  Ceiling Test

   A requested successor Authority Set is in-ceiling when every one of
   its entries is a subset of some authority_ceiling entry under the
   issuance profile's subset rule
   ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission]).  A constraints-bounded
   ceiling uses the same subset semantics.

   A Mission's Authority Set need not lie within its ceiling.  The
   ceiling test covers the complete requested successor Authority Set,
   so a successor that retains authority outside the ceiling requires a
   fresh human approval unless an explicit re-consent has brought that
   authority within the ceiling (Section 3.1.6).

   A ceiling entry MAY name a resource family rather than a single
   resource, under the same resource-narrowing semantics the subset rule
   fixes: a successor entry's resource is in-ceiling when it narrows the
   ceiling entry's.

   Where a drawdown request is triggered by the agent encountering a
   resource not named at approval, and the resource self-declares its
   operations and consequences in a content-addressed form, the
   adjudication MUST evaluate the declaration against the ceiling, and
   the record of any successor the request creates, by policy or by a
   fresh human approval, carries the declaration's digest (Section 5).
   An AAuth deployment adopting Rich Resource Requests composes one such
   self-declaring substrate ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-mission-aauth]).  The
   discovery companion ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-mission-discovery])
   defines the encounter adjudication contract, the identity pinning,
   and the floors this drawdown path carries.

   The Mission Issuer MUST NOT policy-adjudicate a requested authority
   that is not within the consented authority_ceiling.  Such a request
   falls back to an ordinary, freshly human-approved expansion
   (Section 3.1.2), subject to the Mission Issuer's policy, which can
   refuse it as it can refuse any expansion.  A refusal MAY carry
   out_of_ceiling (Section 4); a response that starts the human approval
   never carries it.  A refusal surfaces as the expansion profile maps a
   denial for each completion mode
   ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission-expansion], Section "Expansion
   Denial Reasons").  Approving that authority does not raise the
   ceiling (Section 3.1.6).

   In the example scenario (Section 2.1), the agent finds an adjustment
   of USD 1,200 at 09:18:00.  The client presents the access token
   issued at 09:15:00 and requests a successor whose journal-write entry
   allows USD 2,000, which is within no ceiling entry.  The request
   falls back to a fresh human approval.  The following is an example of
   the response, the expansion profile's deferred response with no
   denial reason; no Mission is created yet, and
   msn_uG1kkHwXSlzUs4OKztGGk5FOmBnkpTOb stays active:

   HTTP/1.1 400 Bad Request
   Content-Type: application/json
   Cache-Control: no-store

   {
     "error": "authorization_pending",
     "deferral_code": "dfc_mgumqI7ZWFKY71J9GLLeo7",
     "expires_in": 600,
     "interval": 5
   }

   If alice approves the USD 2,000 entry without re-consenting the
   ceiling, the successor carries the ceiling unchanged, and the USD
   2,000 entry stays outside it (Section 3.1.6).

3.1.2.  Policy Adjudication

   When the predecessor consented to a drawdown policy that authorizes
   the requested widening, the Mission Issuer MAY satisfy the
   adjudication's approval event by policy rather than by a fresh human
   approval, exactly as a parent Mission's Authority Set may permit
   policy-approved child creation
   ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission-child-delegation]).  This is an
   explicit override of the expansion profile's consent step for the in-
   ceiling case only.

   This does not widen authority without consent
   ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission-expansion]).  The consent is the
   human consent given at the initial approval to the ceiling and the
   drawdown policy; policy adjudication only draws within that pre-given
   consent and can never exceed the ceiling.

   An in-ceiling request the drawdown policy does not authorize is not
   refused with out_of_ceiling; it falls back to an ordinary, freshly
   human-approved expansion.  A drawdown policy snapshot that does not
   match drawdown_policy.digest authorizes nothing, so a request under
   it falls back the same way.  A drawdown request that falls back to a
   fresh human approval completes deferred or interactively, as any
   expansion does.

3.1.3.  Conditions That Require a Fresh Approval

   *Prohibited classes.* Some authority classes always require a fresh
   human approval even within the ceiling.  To make that testable, a
   deployment MUST publish in the Mission Deployment Profile a mapping
   from its action identifiers to the runtime profile's action classes
   ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-mission-runtime]), or an equivalent declared
   classification.

   A drawdown request MUST be adjudicated by a fresh human approval when
   it:

   *  grants authority in the irreversible, external-commitment, or
      privileged-administration class;

   *  satisfies the runtime profile's external-communication predicate
      (the exfiltration leg, [I-D.draft-mcguinness-mission-runtime]); or

   *  grants cross-domain authority.

   The drawdown policy MUST NOT permit policy-only adjudication of such
   a drawdown request.

   *Live Child Missions.* Policy adjudication skips the interactive
   prompt, and with it the expansion profile's child-cascade consent
   notice ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission-expansion]), while
   supersession terminally cascades to the predecessor's non-terminal
   Child Missions
   ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission-child-delegation]).  The Mission
   Issuer therefore MUST NOT policy-adjudicate an in-ceiling drawdown
   request while the predecessor has non-terminal Child Missions: such a
   request falls back to a fresh human approval, at which that notice
   can be rendered.

   *Discharged authority.* The Mission Issuer MUST NOT policy-adjudicate
   a successor whose complete Authority Set, including authority carried
   forward from its predecessor, overlaps in authority the authority a
   discharge restriction of its expansion chain covers
   ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission-discharge]), whatever its
   drawdown policy permits.  The test is overlap in authority, not
   structural equality or entry identity.  Such a request falls back to
   a fresh human approval whose consent disclosure names the discharged
   entry and the condition that discharged it.

   The Mission Issuer MUST apply this test to every drawdown request in
   the chain after the discharge, not only to one from the Mission that
   holds the discharged entry, and neither an approval of unrelated
   authority nor a ceiling review clears the restriction (Section 3.2).
   Policy adjudication cannot restore discharged authority.

   *Contained authority.* The Mission Issuer MUST NOT policy-adjudicate
   a successor whose complete Authority Set, including authority carried
   forward from its predecessor, overlaps in authority capability under
   a containment restriction of its expansion chain
   ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission-containment]), whatever its
   drawdown policy permits.  Such a request falls back to a fresh human
   approval whose consent disclosure names the contained capability and
   the event class that caused its containment.

   The Mission Issuer MUST apply this test to every drawdown request in
   the chain after the containment, not only to one from the Mission
   that holds the overlay, and neither an approval of unrelated
   authority nor a ceiling review clears the restriction (Section 3.2).
   A drawdown request whose authority overlaps no restricted capability
   remains eligible for policy adjudication under the other guards of
   Section 3.1.  Contained authority therefore cannot be restored by
   policy.

   Both tests are re-applied at successor activation, so a containment
   or discharge that lands after adjudication is caught there
   ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission-expansion], Section "Concurrent
   Expansion Reconciliation").

   *Review cadence.* A drawdown request for a chain past its ceiling
   review cadence also falls back to a fresh human approval
   (Section 3.2).

3.1.4.  Drawdown Bounds

   Policy adjudication is bounded per drawdown request and across the
   chain, so a pre-consented ceiling cannot become a standing grant that
   a compromised agent reaches through unattended drawdown requests.

   A single policy-adjudicated in-ceiling drawdown MUST NOT activate the
   entire remaining ceiling.  A drawdown request would activate the
   entire remaining ceiling unless the Mission Issuer can show a request
   the ceiling admits that no entry of the successor's Authority Set
   authorizes: one of an authority_ceiling entry's actions, with
   resource and constraint values that entry admits
   ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission], Section "Subset Rule").  The
   successor's entries are judged together, so ceiling authority it
   splits across several entries counts as held.  Policy adjudication
   therefore never reaches the entire ceiling: the final increment
   always takes a fresh human approval.

   The drawdown policy MUST bound each policy-adjudicated drawdown
   incrementally, by one or both of:

   *  a per-drawdown delta bound; or

   *  narrowing the successor's Authority Set to what the triggering
      action needs.

   The concrete per-drawdown bound MUST be published in the Mission
   Deployment Profile.

   Each policy-adjudicated drawdown creates a successor Mission, so a
   per-Mission bound would reset at every step.  A deployment:

   *  MUST rate-bound policy-adjudicated drawdowns per expansion chain,
      keyed by the chain's root Mission and counted across predecessor
      links per unit time;

   *  MUST publish the concrete rate bound in the Mission Deployment
      Profile; and

   *  MUST record each policy-adjudicated drawdown as an approval event
      whose decision mechanism is the drawdown policy that authorized it
      (Section 5).

   A drawdown request that would activate the entire remaining ceiling,
   or that exceeds the per-drawdown bound or the chain rate bound, falls
   back to a fresh human approval, exactly as a request the drawdown
   policy does not authorize does (Section 3.1.2).

3.1.5.  The Successor Mission

   The successor is created as the expansion profile requires: its
   Authority Set freshly derived and bound by the ceiling, its
   predecessor member set, the predecessor superseded.  The successor's
   subject and authority_source are the predecessor's, and the commit
   that activates it checks that source's ceiling
   ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission-expansion], Section "Successor
   Subject and Authority Source").

   A policy-adjudicated successor MUST carry the predecessor's
   authority_ceiling and drawdown_policy unchanged or narrowed,
   committed under the same or, when narrowed, a recomputed
   ceiling_hash.  Any change to either beyond narrowing requires an
   explicit re-consent (Section 3.1.6), never policy adjudication.  The
   successor MUST carry ceiling_consent_hash, and the instant and
   Approver of that consent, unchanged: a narrowing is not a consent, so
   it resets neither the review cadence (Section 3.2) nor the cumulative
   drawdown count (Section 5).

   A drawdown request that passes every policy-adjudication guard of
   Section 3.1 completes synchronously in the token-exchange response
   ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission-expansion], Section "The
   Expansion Request"), which returns the successor's access token.  The
   successor is still created through the full approval-event machinery
   of the expansion profile, under the ceiling_drawdown basis
   (Section 3.1.7); only the interactive prompt is skipped.  The Mission
   Issuer MUST commit the successor's activation, the predecessor's
   supersession, and the creation reservation, applying the expansion
   profile's activation checks in that same atomic step, before it
   delivers the response.  A lost response is recovered through
   creation_request_id ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission-expansion],
   Section "Creation Idempotency").

   In the example scenario (Section 2.1), the agent finds an adjustment
   of USD 180 to post at 09:15:00.  Its Mission lacks journal-
   entries.write, so the client requests an expansion as an [RFC8693]
   token exchange, proving possession of the Mission's access token with
   a DPoP proof [RFC9449].  The mission_intent carries the baseline Q3
   Intent.  The following is an example of the request; client
   authentication is omitted, and extra line breaks are for display
   only:

   POST /token HTTP/1.1
   Host: as.example.com
   Content-Type: application/x-www-form-urlencoded
   DPoP: eyJ0eXAiOiJkcG9wK2p3dCIsImFsZyI6IkVTMjU2Iiwi...

   grant_type=urn%3Aietf%3Aparams%3Aoauth%3Agrant-type%3Atoken-exchange&
   requested_token_type=
     urn%3Aietf%3Aparams%3Aoauth%3Atoken-type%3Aaccess_token&
   subject_token=<the%20Mission%27s%20access%20token>&
   subject_token_type=
     urn%3Aietf%3Aparams%3Aoauth%3Atoken-type%3Aaccess_token&
   mission_intent=%7B%22intent%22%3A%7B...%7D%7D&

   and the journal-write entry, each a ceiling entry.  It adds one

3.1.6.  Ceiling Re-Consent

   A fresh human approval in a chain that carries a ceiling, whether the
   request fell back from policy adjudication or lay outside the
   ceiling, makes two separate decisions: the Approver approves the
   successor's authority and can, separately, re-consent the ceiling.

   Without an explicit re-consent, the successor MUST carry the
   predecessor's authority_ceiling, drawdown_policy, ceiling_hash and
   ceiling_consent_hash unchanged, together with the instant and the
   Approver of the ceiling consent ceiling_consent_hash commits, and the
   ceiling review cadence does not restart.  Authority approved beyond
   the ceiling stays outside it: a later expansion whose Authority Set
   retains that authority is outside the ceiling (Section 3.1.1).

   Where the Approver explicitly re-consents a ceiling and drawdown
   policy at that approval, the successor MUST record the re-consented
   authority_ceiling and the selected drawdown_policy under a recomputed
   ceiling_hash, record that hash as its ceiling_consent_hash, and
   record the re-consent's instant and Approver as those of the ceiling
   consent.  The ceiling review cadence restarts from that instant
   (Section 3.2).  The consent disclosure MUST render the re-consented
   ceiling and policy as at the initial approval (Section 3).  A re-
   consent is a ceiling review, so its disclosure also renders the
   chain's record that Section 3.2 requires.

   Either way the expansion chain continues: its root, predecessor
   links, rate-bound window, cumulative drawdown count, and every
   discharge and containment restriction carry forward, and a re-consent
   clears none of them.

3.1.7.  The ceiling_drawdown Approval Basis

   A policy-adjudicated in-ceiling successor is rooted in a standing-
   consent approval_basis, under the issuance profile's open type set
   ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission]), with type ceiling_drawdown,
   defined and owned by this profile.  Its members are:

   *  consent_principal: the Approver who consented the ceiling;

   *  activation: an object with exactly two members, policy_id (the
      Mission's drawdown_policy.id) and policy_version (its
      drawdown_policy.version, the version whose content
      drawdown_policy.digest commits and this adjudication evaluated);

   *  activation_actor: the requesting client;

   *  adjudication: REQUIRED for this basis, the decision mechanism
      ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission], Section "Principals and
      Approval Basis"): kind policy, and a policy whose id and version
      are activation's policy_id and policy_version, the drawdown policy
      this adjudication evaluated;

   *  root_commitment: the predecessor's ceiling_consent_hash, the
      latest explicitly human-consented ceiling and drawdown policy,
      never a ceiling_hash a policy narrowing recomputed; and

   *  approved_at (the issuance profile's standing-consent requirement):
      the instant the Approver consented the ceiling that
      root_commitment commits, read from the Mission Issuer's retained
      record of that consent, never from the drawdown request.

   The successor record's approval_event_id identifies this policy-
   adjudicated drawdown, never the ceiling consent: a unique value the
   Mission Issuer allocates when it creates the exchange's (client,
   creation_request_id) reservation and retains with the committed
   successor ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission-expansion],
   Section "Creation Idempotency").  A retry recovers it; a reservation
   created after that key's tombstone expires receives a new one.

   The following is an example of the scenario's successor record
   (Section 2.1), other members omitted:

   {
     "id": "msn_uG1kkHwXSlzUs4OKztGGk5FOmBnkpTOb",
     "issuer": "https://as.example.com",
     "predecessor": "msn_pasG9An7Ivj9cOdd3RqWWonuaLAbDHtM",
     "subject": { "iss": "https://idp.example.com",
       "sub": "user_3p2q8mN1a0kV7tR" },
     "approval_basis": {
       "type": "ceiling_drawdown",
       "consent_principal": { "iss": "https://idp.example.com",
         "sub": "user_3p2q8mN1a0kV7tR" },
       "activation": { "policy_id": "q3-reconcile-drawdown",
         "policy_version": "1" },
       "activation_actor": { "iss": "https://as.example.com",
         "sub": "s6BhdRkqt3" },
       "adjudication": { "kind": "policy",
         "policy": { "id": "q3-reconcile-drawdown", "version": "1" } },
       "root_commitment":
         "sha-256:8PkE9J8vIWBKyPTG21yYer3W3BPP8opLI2moZr3S-kw",
       "approved_at": "2026-11-02T08:00:00Z"
     },
     "approval_event_id": "ape_1hJEKlr4vhnTLqwrYhpX",
     "ceiling_hash":
       "sha-256:8PkE9J8vIWBKyPTG21yYer3W3BPP8opLI2moZr3S-kw",
     "ceiling_consent_hash":
       "sha-256:8PkE9J8vIWBKyPTG21yYer3W3BPP8opLI2moZr3S-kw",
     "drawdown_count": 1,
     "created_at": "2026-11-02T09:15:00Z"
   }

   approved_at is the instant of the ceiling consent, not of the policy-
   adjudicated drawdown.  The successor carries the predecessor's
   authority_ceiling and drawdown_policy unchanged, so its ceiling_hash
   is the predecessor's and equals ceiling_consent_hash, the
   root_commitment.

   The child delegation profile's policy_drawdown basis
   ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission-child-delegation]) is not
   reused: that value names policy-approved child creation, not a
   ceiling drawdown.

   The issuance profile's fourth high-risk class, a consumption bound
   ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission], Section "Approver
   Authentication Strength"), is not held back here.  A successor
   continues its predecessor's consumption counters, and a policy-
   adjudicated successor cannot raise a bound
   ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-mission-metering]), so a policy-adjudicated
   drawdown widens authority within the ceiling without granting new
   capacity.

   The ceiling_drawdown approval_basis already carries the trace the
   issuance profile's approval-authentication floor requires for a
   consumption bound, through the consent_principal and approved_at
   fields this section defines.  A deployment recording Consent Evidence
   renders the bound at the same surface under the metering profile's
   consent-integrity rule ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-mission-metering]).

   Where a deployment adopts Approval Governance
   ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-mission-approval-governance]) and records a
   Governance Record for a policy-adjudicated drawdown, the
   ceiling_drawdown approval_basis satisfies that profile's accountable-
   approver rule directly, through the same consent_principal,
   root_commitment, and approved_at this document already requires.  No
   assertion is fabricated in the name of the requesting client or the
   drawdown policy to stand in for a fresh human decision that did not
   occur.  Approval Governance's own high-risk-class default still binds
   that record: a policy-adjudicated drawdown carrying a consumption
   bound activates only where a committed, class-named exception admits
   it; absent the exception, the Mission fails to activate under that
   profile's atomic-commitment rule.

3.1.8.  Derivation Limits

   This section binds only a deployment that also adopts Mission
   Derivation Limits
   ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission-derivation-limits]).  A policy-
   adjudicated drawdown continues its predecessor's undertaking, so it
   cannot replenish the predecessor's allowance of counted derivations:

   *  The successor MUST carry forward the predecessor's committed
      derivation count.  Where the predecessor has a derivation_limit,
      the successor's derivation_limit MUST NOT exceed it: a stricter
      authorization-server policy ceiling or requested_derivation_limit
      narrows it further, and omitting or raising the request neither
      removes nor raises it.  A predecessor without a derivation_limit
      has no finite limit to pass on, and the successor's is established
      as Mission Derivation Limits states
      ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission-derivation-limits],
      Section "Effective Limit").

   *  The Mission Issuer MUST read the predecessor's committed count and
      transfer it in the atomic step above that activates the successor,
      supersedes the predecessor, and commits the creation reservation,
      after any predecessor derivation that commits first.  Concurrent
      derivation under the predecessor and competing or duplicate
      drawdown requests MUST NOT fork or replenish the remaining
      allowance.

   *  The successor access token the drawdown response returns is one
      derivation, charged to the successor in that same atomic step and
      never again to the predecessor.  Where the carried-forward count
      plus that derivation would exceed the successor's
      derivation_limit, the Mission Issuer MUST refuse the drawdown
      request as Mission Derivation Limits refuses a derivation
      ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission-derivation-limits],
      Section "Enforcement at Issuance"), with invalid_grant and its
      mission_error diagnostic, creating no successor, superseding no
      predecessor, and counting nothing.

   *  An exact creation retry MUST return the recorded result without
      repeating its accounting; returning the existing access token adds
      no derivation.  A fresh delivery token minted on recovery is
      ordinary issuance ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission-expansion],
      Section "Recovery Is Delivery"), counted against and gated by the
      successor's derivation_limit.

   The ceiling consent's disclosure (Section 3) MUST state this
   inheritance, as Mission Derivation Limits requires of a standing
   consent ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission-derivation-limits],
   Section "Approval Rendering"): policy-adjudicated successors carry
   forward the Mission's derivation count and never exceed its rendered
   derivation_limit, or, where the Mission has none, no finite limit is
   guaranteed for them.

   A successor created by a fresh human approval establishes its limit
   afresh, as Mission Derivation Limits states.  This bounds counted
   derivations through the drawdown succession only, not a Mission's
   descendants, other tokens, or resource effects.

3.2.  The Ceiling Review

   A pre-consented ceiling is standing consent.  Standing consent
   requires periodic human review, so this profile bounds the chain in
   time, not only per drawdown request and per unit time
   (Section 3.1.4).

   A deployment MUST publish a *ceiling review cadence* in its Mission
   Deployment Profile.  The Mission Issuer MUST NOT policy-adjudicate an
   in-ceiling drawdown request for a chain whose ceiling consent, the
   initial consent or the latest re-consent, is older than that cadence:
   the request falls back to a fresh human approval, exactly as a
   request the drawdown policy does not authorize does (Section 3.1.2).

   The cadence is a recency ceiling on the ceiling_drawdown approval
   basis's approved_at, the kind of declared maximum standing-consent
   age the issuance profile permits a deployment to declare
   ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission]); this profile makes publishing
   one mandatory rather than optional.

   The cadence is part of the drawdown policy's own versioned content: a
   deployment's drawdown policy document MUST state the cadence
   effective under a given version, identified by the policy_id and
   policy_version the ceiling_drawdown activation carries
   (Section 3.1.7), per the issuance profile's own rule that a mutable,
   unversioned value MUST NOT serve this role.

   Because ceiling_hash commits that policy's digest, the cadence is
   anchored with it.  Changing the cadence changes the policy snapshot,
   so a drawdown request under the changed policy no longer matches the
   committed digest and falls back to a fresh human approval until a
   review re-consents the ceiling under the new policy.  An auditor
   recovers the cadence that governed a given policy-adjudicated
   drawdown from the retained snapshot that digest names, exactly as it
   resolves the drawdown logic itself.

   A ceiling review is a fresh human approval that explicitly re-
   consents the ceiling and drawdown policy, a narrowed ceiling included
   (Section 3.1.6).  No request asks for a review on its own: a review
   rides a fresh human approval of an expansion in the chain.

   An overdue cadence sends the chain's next widening to a fresh human
   approval but does not require a re-consent.  Where the Approver
   approves only the successor's authority, the ceiling consent stays
   overdue and policy adjudication stays unavailable.  A ceiling
   narrowed by a policy-adjudicated drawdown (Section 3.1.5) has not
   been reviewed: the narrowing keeps the instant of the consent it
   narrows, and the cadence does not restart.

   Every re-consent is a review, including one before the cadence
   expires.  Its consent disclosure MUST render the chain's record since
   the prior review or, for the chain's first review, since the initial
   ceiling consent:

   *  the count and rate of policy-adjudicated drawdowns;

   *  the guard exceptions escalated to human approval;

   *  the out_of_ceiling refusals;

   *  the entries discharged under the Entry Discharge companion's
      completion machinery
      ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission-discharge]), and the chain's
      outstanding discharge restrictions, which re-consenting the
      ceiling does not clear;

   *  the chain's outstanding containment restrictions, each with its
      contained capability and the event class that caused it
      ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission-containment]), which re-
      consenting the ceiling does not clear; and

   *  where the metering profile runs, consumption against its bounds
      ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-mission-metering]).

   Where consent evidence is claimed, that disclosure is committed like
   any other ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission-consent-evidence]), so
   the record shows the reviewer saw the cycle they renewed.
   Section 7.5 lists the signs in the chain's records that a reviewer
   checks.

3.3.  Realizing an Approved Access Request

   Progressive authorization grows authority that a deployment
   anticipated well enough to express as a ceiling.  The runtime
   enforcement layer handles the unanticipated case: it can let an agent
   request authority it discovers it needs at the point of use, through
   an access-request and approval workflow
   ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-mission-runtime]).  That workflow yields a
   permit for the single re-evaluated action.

   To persist the newly approved authority for the rest of the task,
   rather than have the agent re-request it on every call, the Mission
   Issuer MAY realize an approved access request as an expansion:

   *  a request whose authority is within the Mission's consented
      ceiling is realized as a policy-adjudicated drawdown
      (Section 3.1); and

   *  a request whose authority exceeds the ceiling is realized only on
      the fresh human approval the request carries, as an ordinary
      expansion (Section 3.1.1); the ceiling rises only where the
      Approver also re-consents it (Section 3.1.6).

   Realizing a request as an expansion is subject to every rule of the
   expansion profile: the successor's authority is freshly derived and
   bound, the predecessor is superseded, and authority is never widened
   without the consent the request carries
   ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission-expansion]).  An access request
   not realized as an expansion grants only the single runtime permit
   and no durable Mission authority.

4.  The out_of_ceiling Denial Reason

   This document extends the expansion profile's closed set of expansion
   denial reasons ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission-expansion]) by
   specification, as that profile's IANA considerations anticipate, with
   one value, carried in mission_denial_reason exactly as that profile's
   reasons are:

   out_of_ceiling:  The requested authority is not within the Mission's
      consented authority ceiling (Section 3), and the Mission Issuer
      refused the expansion instead of taking it to a fresh human
      approval (Section 3.1.1).

   A consumer that does not implement this document treats
   out_of_ceiling as it treats any unrecognized reason code: the
   expansion stays denied.

   The expansion profile surfaces a denial on the token-exchange
   response as an OAuth error with the reason code in
   mission_denial_reason
   ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission-expansion], Section "Expansion
   Denial Reasons").  Had the Mission Issuer's policy refused the
   scenario's request at 09:18:00 (Section 3.1.1) instead of taking it
   to alice, the following is an example of a refusal that carries the
   code:

   HTTP/1.1 400 Bad Request
   Content-Type: application/json
   Cache-Control: no-store

   {
     "error": "invalid_request",
     "mission_denial_reason": "out_of_ceiling"
   }

5.  Audit Linkage

   Each policy-adjudicated drawdown is an approval event and MUST be
   recorded as one, in which:

   *  the decision mechanism is the drawdown policy rather than a human,
      recorded as the basis's adjudication (Section 3.1.7);

   *  the Approver who consented the ceiling remains the accountable
      consent_principal ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission],
      Section "Role Mapping"); and

   *  the successor's predecessor member links the drawdown chain for an
      authorized auditor exactly as for human-approved expansions
      ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission-expansion]).

   Every Mission record in a chain that carries a ceiling MUST carry the
   chain's cumulative drawdown count as drawdown_count, a nonnegative
   integer counting the chain's committed policy-adjudicated drawdowns,
   so the rate bound of Section 3.1.4 is auditable from the records
   alone.  It is zero on the chain's root Mission, whose approval
   consented the ceiling, and each committed ceiling_drawdown
   successor's is one more than its predecessor's.  A retried or refused
   request leaves it unchanged, and a human-approved successor carries
   it unchanged, a re-consent included; neither a review nor a narrowing
   resets it.

   A deployment MUST retain, with every Mission record in the chain for
   the audit horizon, its authority_ceiling, drawdown_policy,
   ceiling_hash and ceiling_consent_hash, and the consented
   authority_ceiling and drawdown_policy that each ceiling_consent_hash
   commits.  An auditor can then verify that every policy-adjudicated
   drawdown was within the consented envelope and, by resolving the
   retained drawdown policy document at the policy_version each one's
   ceiling_drawdown approval basis already records, that it was
   adjudicated within the cadence that applied at the time
   (Section 3.2).

   The record of every successor whose adjudication, by policy or by a
   fresh human approval, evaluated a resource's self-declaration
   (Section 3.1.1) MUST carry the declaration's digest as
   resource_declaration_digest, so the encounter is reproducible in
   audit: what the resource claimed to be when authority bound to it.  A
   refused request creates no successor and so no such record.

6.  Conformance

   A Mission Issuer that claims *Expansion with Progressive
   Authorization* is a conforming expansion-capable Mission Issuer
   ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission-expansion]) and MUST:

   *  for a Mission whose Approver consented to a ceiling, record the
      consented authority_ceiling and drawdown_policy on the Mission and
      commit them with ceiling_hash, recording that hash as
      ceiling_consent_hash (Section 3);

   *  render the ceiling, and the fact that in-ceiling expansion is
      policy-adjudicated, in the initial approval's consent disclosure
      (Section 3);

   *  evaluate a requested successor Authority Set as in-ceiling by the
      subset rule, never policy-adjudicate an out-of-ceiling request,
      and, where a drawdown request is triggered by a resource's
      content-addressed self-declaration, evaluate the declaration
      against the ceiling (Section 3.1.1);

   *  for a policy-adjudicated successor, carry the predecessor's
      ceiling members unchanged or narrowed and its ceiling_consent_hash
      unchanged, and commit its activation, the predecessor's
      supersession and the creation reservation in one atomic step
      before the token-exchange response (Section 3.1.5);

   *  record a policy-adjudicated successor's ceiling_drawdown approval
      basis, its REQUIRED adjudication included and its root_commitment
      the ceiling_consent_hash, and an approval_event_id that identifies
      that drawdown (Section 3.1.7);

   *  for a successor that a fresh human approval creates in a chain
      that carries a ceiling, carry the predecessor's ceiling members
      unchanged unless the Approver explicitly re-consents a ceiling and
      drawdown policy, record a re-consent under a recomputed
      ceiling_hash, as the new ceiling_consent_hash, with its own
      instant and Approver, render the re-consented ceiling and policy
      in that approval's disclosure, and preserve the chain's history
      and restrictions (Section 3.1.6);

   *  bound each policy-adjudicated drawdown incrementally, so a single
      one never activates the entire remaining ceiling, and publish the
      concrete per-drawdown bound in the Mission Deployment Profile
      (Section 3.1.4);

   *  fall back to a fresh human approval for a drawdown request that
      would activate the entire remaining ceiling, or that exceeds the
      per-drawdown bound or the chain rate bound (Section 3.1.4);

   *  enforce the prohibited-class rule, requiring a fresh human
      approval for a drawdown request that grants irreversible,
      external-commitment, or privileged-administration authority, that
      satisfies the external-communication predicate, or that grants
      cross-domain authority, and publish the action-class mapping in
      the Mission Deployment Profile (Section 3.1.3);

   *  require a fresh human approval for an in-ceiling drawdown request
      while the predecessor has non-terminal Child Missions
      (Section 3.1.3);

   *  require a fresh human approval for a drawdown request whose
      complete Authority Set overlaps authority under a containment or
      discharge restriction of its chain, from any Mission in the chain,
      and name the discharged entry or contained capability, with its
      cause, in that approval's disclosure (Section 3.1.3);

   *  rate-bound policy-adjudicated drawdowns per expansion chain, keyed
      by the chain's root Mission and counted across predecessor links,
      publish the concrete rate bound in the Mission Deployment Profile,
      and record each as an approval event carrying the chain's
      cumulative drawdown count (Section 3.1.4, Section 5);

   *  carry drawdown_count on every Mission record of a chain that
      carries a ceiling, and record resource_declaration_digest on every
      successor whose adjudication, by policy or by a fresh human
      approval, evaluated a resource's self-declaration (Section 5);

   *  as a deployment obligation, retain the consented ceiling members
      with every Mission record of the chain for the audit horizon
      (Section 5);

   *  publish a ceiling review cadence in the Mission Deployment
      Profile, bound each chain by it, refusing policy adjudication past
      it, rendering the chain's record since the prior review, or since
      the initial ceiling consent, in every re-consent's disclosure, and
      stating the cadence effective under each drawdown policy version
      in that policy's own versioned content (Section 3.2);

   *  as a deployment obligation, review and version the drawdown policy
      like approval policy (Section 7.2); and

   *  where the deployment also adopts Mission Derivation Limits, carry
      a policy-adjudicated drawdown's derivation count and limit forward
      without letting concurrent derivations or competing or duplicate
      drawdown requests fork or replenish the allowance, count its
      successor access token once, return the recorded result to an
      exact creation retry without repeating its accounting, refuse a
      drawdown request that would exceed the limit without creating a
      successor or superseding the predecessor, and state this
      inheritance in the ceiling consent's disclosure (Section 3.1.8).

   This checklist summarizes the body.  A requirement it omits still
   applies, with the role, strength and applicability its section gives
   it.

7.  Security Considerations

   The expansion profile's security considerations apply in full
   ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission-expansion]).  This document adds
   the drawdown surface.

7.1.  What Progressive Authorization Bounds

   The ceiling is broad by construction, since it must cover the open-
   ended task.  What stays narrow is the active authority any single
   Mission in the chain yields: each in-ceiling successor is derived for
   the authority actually needed at that step and is independently gated
   and revocable.

   The ceiling is consented once and drawn on many times, so a
   compromised agent can make in-ceiling drawdown requests unattended.
   It cannot instantly wield the ceiling: it can exercise only the
   current active authority, and each drawdown request it makes is
   subject to the per-drawdown and chain rate bounds (Section 3.1.4),
   held to the prohibited-class rule (Section 3.1.3), taken to a fresh
   human approval when the drawdown policy does not authorize it
   (Section 3.1.2), recorded for audit (Section 5), and enforced per
   action by the runtime layer ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-mission-runtime]).

   Progressive authorization bounds, and does not eliminate, standing-
   authority exposure.  A deployment SHOULD pair it with short successor
   lifetimes, constraint-bounded ceilings, and runtime enforcement.

7.2.  The Drawdown Policy as Governance

   The drawdown policy is authority-bearing governance.  A misconfigured
   policy over-grants within the ceiling; it MUST be reviewed and
   versioned like approval policy, and its identity and version are
   recorded as the decision mechanism (Section 5).

7.3.  The Ceiling as a Consent Artifact

   The ceiling is a consent artifact: every policy-adjudicated drawdown
   it later admits acts on the ceiling consent, given at the initial
   approval or at the latest re-consent.  Section 3 and Section 3.1.6
   require that approval's disclosure to render the ceiling and the fact
   that in-ceiling expansion is policy-adjudicated.  Without this
   disclosure, the Approver has not knowingly consented to the ceiling.

7.4.  Probing the Ceiling

   A refusal that carries out_of_ceiling tells the requester that its
   request fell outside the ceiling, so repeated requests can map the
   ceiling.  Omitting the code keeps the ceiling undisclosed
   (Section 3.1.1).  The expansion profile's per-predecessor rate limit
   bounds such probing either way
   ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission-expansion], Section "Policy
   Probing").

7.5.  Periodic Review

   Standing consent requires periodic human review even when nothing is
   misconfigured: the per-drawdown and rate bounds cap rate, not
   duration, and the ceiling review (Section 3.2) is the temporal bound.
   The chain's own records show when the consent no longer matches the
   work:

   *  reviews that never narrow the ceiling;

   *  ceilings that only grow;

   *  guard exceptions trending toward zero because the policy was
      widened; and

   *  discharge that never fires because no unit of work is scoped
      tightly enough to complete.

   The review uses the chain evidence listed in Ceiling Review
   (Section 3.2).

8.  Privacy Considerations

   The ceiling discloses, at initial approval time, the full envelope a
   task may grow into, which can reveal more about the anticipated task
   than any single Mission's Authority Set.

   The expansion profile's predecessor-chain correlation considerations
   apply to the drawdown chain
   ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission-expansion], Section 
   "Predecessor-Chain Correlation").  Access to the ceiling and drawdown
   records SHOULD be scoped to parties with a governance need.

9.  IANA Considerations

   This document requests one registration (Section 9.1).
   authority_ceiling, drawdown_policy, ceiling_consent_hash,
   drawdown_count, and resource_declaration_digest are Mission record
   members defined by this profile, and the mission-authority-ceiling
   anchor typ follows the issuance profile's collision-resistant typ
   convention; none of these requires registration.

Mission Approval Governance

Abstract

Mission-Bound Authorization records one accountable Approver for each Mission and leaves multi-party approval and approval-authority provenance to a governance layer. This document defines that layer's record, the Approval Governance Record: an issuer-signed, issuer-retained record of the policy, assertions, and evaluation behind one approval event. Before the Mission is created, the record is part of the approval decision; afterwards, it is immutable evidence of who approved, under which authority, and why the decision satisfied governance. The record never appears on tokens or protocol messages, and the Mission's single accountable Approver is unchanged.¶

This document also defines the governed_policy approval basis, under which a policy version that an accountable human approved activates Mission proposals, and the Approval Context Commitment, a digest over a Mission's immutable creation facts that lets two authenticated artifacts be matched to one approval.¶

1. Introduction

Mission-Bound Authorization records one accountable Approver for each Mission and leaves multi-party approval and approval-authority provenance to a governance layer. Mission-Bound Authorization for OAuth 2.0 [I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission] (the "issuance profile") states this rule for the OAuth binding, which records the Approver in approval_basis.consent_principal; this document consumes that binding's record members.¶

An enterprise review process is often that governance layer. One approval can involve several principals, a threshold or separation-of-duty rule, and a delegation-of-authority policy. An auditor later has to prove not only who approved, but under which authority the approval was valid.¶

The issuance profile answers who is accountable for a Mission. This document answers under which authority that approval was made, and why it satisfied governance. It defines:¶

  • the Approval Governance Record (AGR), the record of the policy, assertions, and evaluation behind one approval event (Section 5);¶

  • the governed_policy approval basis (Section 11), under which an accountable human approves a bounded policy version once, and that policy then decides eligible Mission proposals, each activation committing a record; and¶

  • the Approval Context Commitment (Section 14), an optional digest over a Mission's immutable creation facts that other authenticated artifacts can disclose.¶

The record has two phases:¶

  • Before commitment, it is part of the approval decision. An assertion that fails authentication, event binding, or policy authorization does not contribute (Section 6), and a record that cannot be evaluated or persisted prevents activation (Section 8).¶

  • After commitment, it is evidence: immutable, signed (Section 9), and consumed by audit, never by enforcement.¶

This document is optional. A deployment that records nothing beyond the Mission Record's accountable approval_basis.consent_principal is fully conformant to its Mission binding and unaffected by this document. A profile can require the record; Section 4 defines the conditions under which one does.¶

The record is independent of the approval ceremony. It applies alike to synchronous approval, to deferred approval ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission-approval]), and to the native asynchronous approval of a standalone Mission Authority Server ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-mission-authority-server]), and it depends on no deferral substrate.¶

This document uses JSON [RFC8259] as the data model for the Approval Governance Record. Its digests are constructed with the issuance profile's commitment mechanisms ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission]) and classified under the substrate's default commitment construction, which this document imports normatively ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-mission-substrate]): approval_policy.digest, approval_context_commitment, and submission_evidence_commitment are envelope anchors, and the record digest (Section 9) is a canonical-object digest.¶

The terms Mission, Mission Record, Mission Issuer, Approver, Subject, approval event, Mission Intent, Authority Set, audit horizon, approval_event_id, intent_hash, and authority_hash are used as defined in [I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission].¶

This document defines the following terms:¶

Approval Governance Record (AGR):

The policy, assertions, and evaluation standing behind one approval event (Section 5).¶

Assertion:

One principal's authenticated decision statement carried in an AGR (Section 5.2).¶

Accountable assertion:

The assertion that satisfies the accountable-approver rule (Section 6.2).¶

Evaluation:

The issuer's determination of whether the recorded assertions satisfy the referenced approval policy (Section 8).¶

Record digest:

The digest over a complete record, envelope included, that every external binding names (Section 9).¶

Recording trigger:

A condition under which a profile requires this record for an approval event (Section 4).¶

Recording Issuer:

A Mission Issuer that records Approval Governance Records (Section 15).¶

Relying Auditor:

A party that verifies a retained record and can re-check its evaluation (Section 15).¶

3. Relationship to the Issuance Profile

The Mission Record carries exactly one accountable principal, approval_basis.consent_principal, and this document does not change that. The accountable Approver remains the only principal that any downstream projection, token, or enforcement point consumes. The record captures the standing behind that approval, never a second authorization surface visible outside the issuer.¶

The approval_event_id member joins the record to the approval event and to the Mission Record it governs ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission]). The record never appears on tokens or in any protocol message; it is issuer-retained control-plane state. OAuth standardizes the authorization data that a client requests and is granted ([RFC9396] for rich authorization requests), not an organization's internal approval provenance, so the record also stays out of enforcement projections, matching the issuance profile's control-plane discipline.¶

The record governs the Mission approval event. An action-time approval under the runtime profile's re-evaluation surfaces ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-mission-runtime]) is a different layer and never joins this record.¶

Where the governed Mission populates approval_basis.adjudication ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission], Section "Role Mapping") and this record exists:¶

  • adjudication.governance_record is true;¶

  • adjudication.kind names the mechanism the approval basis fixes: human for direct, and policy for a standing-consent basis, including governed_policy (Section 11). It is never a value that names this record itself, and never a flattening of its assertion set into a single principal; and¶

  • where the record carries an accountable assertion (Section 6), that assertion's own kind MUST equal adjudication.kind.¶

4. Recording Triggers

A profile MAY require this record; the Enterprise Mission Authority Profile does so under these recording triggers ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-mission-authority-server]). The triggers are defined here so that a profile cites one list. Under a profile that adopts these recording triggers, the record is REQUIRED for an approval event when any of the following holds:¶

  1. the Mission Record's approval_basis.consent_principal differs from its subject;¶

  2. more than one principal contributes to the decision;¶

  3. a non-human assertion contributes to the decision;¶

  4. a threshold, veto, or separation-of-duty rule is evaluated; or¶

  5. validating any assertion requires authority standing outside the Mission Record.¶

A deployment that adopts no profile requiring the record has no obligation to record one merely because a trigger holds. Independently of any profile, every activation under governed_policy records an Approval Governance Record (Section 11). Every Approval Governance Record a deployment records, whether required or not, MUST satisfy the requirements of this document.¶

The Approver is always approval_basis.consent_principal ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission]), so a Mission whose Approver differs from its Subject holds the first trigger, and the Enterprise Mission Authority Profile always records an Approval Governance Record for it.¶

Direct self-approval by one authenticated human is the degenerate case: the Mission Record already carries it completely, and no record is required. This presumes that the human resolved the approval on the approval surface itself. Where an agent resolves it on its own behalf, there is no human decision for this list to be degenerate about ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-mission-runtime]).¶

The following table gives an example approval for each trigger, and for the degenerate case. Each row names one trigger that holds; others can hold too.¶

Table 1: Recording trigger examples
Approval Trigger that holds
A manager approves a Mission whose Subject is Alice The Approver differs from the Subject
Alice and a finance co-approver both approve More than one principal contributes
A delegation-of-authority policy asserts alongside Alice (Section 5.5) A non-human assertion contributes
A two-of-three finance rule decides the approval A threshold rule is evaluated
An approver's authority comes from a delegation matrix held outside the Mission Record Validating an assertion requires outside standing
Alice alone approves her own Mission on the approval surface None; no record is required

5. The Approval Governance Record

An Approval Governance Record is a JSON object. Section 5.1, Section 5.2 and Section 5.3 define its members. Section 6 and Section 8 define how the issuer builds and commits it, and Section 9 defines its integrity protection.¶

5.1. Record Members

approval_event_id:

REQUIRED. A string. The approval event's identifier, equal to the Mission Record's approval_event_id ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission]).¶

mission:

REQUIRED. An object binding the record to the approved Mission: issuer (the Mission Issuer), intent_hash, and authority_hash, each equal to the committed Mission Record member of the same name. A record whose binding does not match the Mission Record it is retained with is invalid.¶

approval_policy:

REQUIRED. An object identifying the governing delegation-of-authority policy: id (an identifier), version (a string), and digest (an integrity anchor over the retained policy snapshot, in the encoded form Section 2 fixes). Section 5.4 defines the digest and the snapshot's retention.¶

assertions:

REQUIRED. An array of one or more decision assertions (Section 5.2).¶

evaluation:

REQUIRED. An object recording the outcome, with these members:¶

decision:

REQUIRED. A string. The only value a committed record carries is approved, since a record exists only for an approval that committed (Section 8).¶

evaluated_at:

REQUIRED. An RFC 3339 timestamp.¶

contributing:

REQUIRED. An array of the assertion_id values the evaluation counted toward the rule the approval satisfied. A denial the policy evaluated that did not block the approval is recorded but not contributing, and a blocking denial leaves no committed record (Section 8), so a deny assertion never appears in contributing.¶

approval_context_commitment:

OPTIONAL. A string. The Approval Context Commitment over the governed Mission's immutable creation facts (Section 14), in the encoded form Section 2 fixes. It is a member of this record, never of the Mission Record (Section 14.3). An issuer that records it computes it in the same commit that creates the Mission and persists this record, before the record is signed (Section 8), so the signed payload commits to it. A verifier checks it against the Mission Record (Section 9).¶

envelope:

REQUIRED. An object. Integrity protection over the members above, in the form Section 9 defines.¶

5.2. Assertion Members

Each element of assertions is an object with these members:¶

assertion_id:

REQUIRED. A string, unique within the record.¶

principal:

REQUIRED. An object with iss and sub: the principal in whose name the assertion is made.¶

  • For a human assertion, the authenticated human who decided.¶

  • For a policy assertion, the human who approved the exact policy version its authority names (where a consented root commits a separate evaluator that decides, the human who approved that root), as the issuer's retained, authenticated governance state records. Where several humans approved that version together, as under dual control, it is the one human that state names as accountable for the approval, as the issuance profile records one accountable Approver ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission], Section "Single Accountable Approver"). The policy is the decision mechanism, never the principal.¶

  • For a service assertion, the service that produced the decision.¶

kind:

REQUIRED. A string, one of human, service, or policy.¶

decision:

REQUIRED. A string, one of approve or deny.¶

decided_at:

REQUIRED. An RFC 3339 [RFC3339] timestamp.¶

authority:

REQUIRED. An object recording the provenance under which the principal was authorized to decide. Its members depend on kind (Section 5.3).¶

reason:

OPTIONAL. A string.¶

5.3. Assertion Authority

An assertion's authority is an object whose members depend on the assertion's kind. Each member below states its type, its presence, and the source the issuer takes it from. A member this section does not define for the assertion's kind carries no semantics under this document.¶

A human assertion's authority records the authentication the issuer verified for the decision (Section 6.1):¶

auth_time:

REQUIRED. An RFC 3339 timestamp: when the principal authenticated for this decision.¶

acr:

A string: the authentication context class reference of that authentication. REQUIRED when the issuer possesses it at the approval event.¶

amr:

An array of strings: the authentication methods references of that authentication. REQUIRED when the issuer possesses them at the approval event.¶

The issuer takes these members from the authentication it verified, never from the requesting client or agent.¶

A policy assertion's authority names the deciding policy version and its human approval:¶

policy_id:

REQUIRED. A string: the identifier of the policy that decided.¶

version:

REQUIRED. A string: the exact version of that policy.¶

approved_at:

REQUIRED. An RFC 3339 timestamp: the human approval instant of that exact policy version, not the assertion instant. The decided_at member records when the policy asserted, and approved_at records when a human approved the version it asserts under.¶

approved_by:

OPTIONAL. An object with iss and sub: the human who approved that version. When present, it MUST equal principal.¶

The issuer takes policy_id and version from the policy it evaluated, or from the authenticated decision it verified. The approved_at and approved_by members are corroborated provenance, never self-authenticating evidence: the issuer MUST verify approved_at from retained, authenticated governance state for the named policy_id and version, and it MUST NOT accept approved_at as the assertion's own uncorroborated clock value (Section 7). It takes approved_by from the same state and MUST NOT accept it from the asserting party.¶

A service assertion's authority identifies the logic that produced the decision, and the model where a model produced it:¶

logic_id:

REQUIRED. A string: the identifier of the logic (for example, a workflow, a rule engine, or a model) that produced the decision.¶

logic_version:

REQUIRED. A string: the version of that logic.¶

model_id:

A string: the identifier of the model that produced the decision. REQUIRED where a model produced it.¶

model_version:

A string: the version of that model. REQUIRED where model_id is present.¶

Where the logic is itself a model, logic_id and logic_version carry the model's identifier and version, as model_id and model_version do. The issuer takes these members from the integrity-protected channel or signature it verified for the assertion (Section 6.1).¶

5.4. Approval Policy Reference

The approval_policy.digest preimage is the issuance profile's integrity-anchor envelope ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission]), canonicalized with JCS [RFC8785], with these members:¶

  • typ: mission-approval-policy, which domain-separates this anchor from every other integrity anchor;¶

  • iss: the Mission Issuer, which prevents cross-issuer replay; and¶

  • value: an object of content_type (the snapshot's media type) and content (the base64url, no-padding encoding of the snapshot bytes).¶

The snapshot bytes and their media type, or the referenced policy version they represent, MUST be retained for the Mission's audit horizon ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission]), so an independent auditor reproduces the digest exactly and re-checks the evaluation against the policy that governed it.¶

Threshold, quorum, veto, and separation-of-duty semantics live in the referenced policy; any human-readable summary of them in deployment tooling is advisory and carries no semantics in this record.¶

6. Assertion Requirements

These rules govern the assertions the issuer evaluates (Section 8).¶

6.1. Assertion Validation

The following rules apply to the assertions in a record:¶

  • Every assertion that contributes to the evaluation MUST be authenticated: a human assertion with the authentication the issuance profile requires of an Approver at an approval event, a service or policy assertion through an integrity-protected channel or signature the issuer verifies.¶

  • Every assertion MUST be bound to this approval event: an assertion is made against this approval_event_id, this intent_hash, and this authority_hash, and an assertion captured for one event MUST NOT be replayed into another. An assertion that can narrow the authority, such as a model output an activation consults, is made against the authority_hash of the set it evaluated rather than of the narrowed result; the issuer records that set with the activation's inputs, and the committed Authority Set lies within it.¶

  • Every contributing assertion MUST be policy-authorized: before counting it, the issuer verifies that the asserting principal was eligible to assert under the referenced approval_policy version. For a policy assertion, that means both that the policy its authority names was eligible to decide under approval_policy and that its principal was eligible to approve that policy version (or, where a consented root commits a separate evaluator that decides, that root).¶

  • Denials and vetoes MUST be recorded: an assertion set that omits a negative assertion misrepresents the decision.¶

An assertion that fails authentication, event binding, or policy authorization MUST NOT contribute to the evaluation and MUST NOT be counted toward any policy rule.¶

6.2. Accountable Assertion

The accountable-approver rule ties a record to the Mission's one accountable principal, the Mission Record's approval_basis.consent_principal. The Mission's approval_basis.type fixes how a record satisfies it:¶

  • For direct, the record MUST carry exactly one human assertion whose principal is consent_principal and whose decision is approve: the human decision a direct approval event carries ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission]). Contributing policy and service assertions do not replace it.¶

  • For governed_policy, the record MUST carry exactly one policy assertion whose principal is consent_principal, whose decision is approve, and whose authority names the deciding policy (Section 11.2). The policy is the decision mechanism, and the principal stays the human who approved it.¶

  • For any other named standing-consent approval_basis ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission]), the Mission satisfies the rule through its authenticated approval_basis record, not through an assertion: consent_principal, root_commitment, and approved_at, fixed at the approval event and immutable, stand in for an accountable assertion. The record can still carry contributing policy and service assertions.¶

A record that does not satisfy the rule for its basis does not commit (Section 8). The assertion that satisfies it under direct or governed_policy is the accountable assertion; no other basis has one. The accountable assertion's kind MUST be human or policy: a service assertion, including one a model produced, is a contributing governance input, never the accountable assertion. Under every basis, the high-risk-class restrictions of Section 7.3 apply.¶

No assertion is fabricated in the name of the activating policy or the requesting actor to stand in for a fresh human decision that did not occur.¶

Where the Mission populates approval_basis.adjudication, the accountable assertion's kind also equals adjudication.kind (Section 3).¶

7. Policy-Approval Recency

This section bounds staleness on the AGR policy assertion path: it governs a policy assertion's authority.approved_at and the kind of the accountable assertion (Section 6.2). It does not apply to a human assertion, whose decided_at is the approval instant itself.¶

7.1. Recency Declarations

A Recording Issuer that admits policy assertions MUST declare a maximum policy-approval age per consequence class (a recency ceiling) and a bounded clock-skew allowance, and MAY declare, per class, an exception admitting a policy assertion as the accountable assertion for that class, each exception carrying its own maximum age.¶

The ceiling, the skew allowance, and any exception MUST be part of the retained approval_policy snapshot that approval_policy.digest commits (Section 5.4), or a separately versioned declaration committed from that snapshot. A mutable, out-of-band deployment statement MUST NOT serve this role: it would make the evaluation impossible to reproduce, contrary to the purpose of the retained policy, which lets an auditor re-run the decision (Section 5.4).¶

7.2. Evaluating Recency

At evaluation, the issuer MUST classify the committed Mission's consequence classes from the derived Authority Set and from any consumption bound the Mission Intent carries ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-mission-metering]). The issuer MUST select, across every class either source carries, the strictest maximum age that applies, accounting for any declared exception. This is a property of the whole committed Mission the record's mission.authority_hash and mission.intent_hash bind to (Section 5.1), never of one assertion or entry in isolation. For a class with a declared exception, the maximum that applies is the smaller of that class's recency ceiling and the exception's maximum age: an exception admits a policy accountable assertion for its class and never lengthens the ceiling.¶

For each policy assertion the evaluation would count toward the applicable maximum, the issuer:¶

  • uses the authority.approved_at it verified under Section 5.3;¶

  • MUST measure the assertion's age as evaluation.evaluated_at minus authority.approved_at, using the atomic-commitment instant (Section 8);¶

  • MUST enforce authority.approved_at <= decided_at <= evaluation.evaluated_at, subject to the declared clock-skew allowance; and¶

  • MUST reject the assertion if authority.approved_at or decided_at is later than the issuer's own clock at evaluation, beyond that allowance.¶

A policy assertion whose age exceeds the applicable maximum, or whose ordering the checks above do not admit, fails policy-authorization under Section 6.1: the issuer MUST NOT count it toward the evaluation, and by Section 8 the Mission MUST NOT be created active on an evaluation that depended on it.¶

An evaluation.evaluated_at later than the issuer's own clock, beyond the declared allowance, is a defect of the record rather than of one assertion: the evaluation MUST NOT be treated as complete, and by Section 8 the Mission MUST NOT be created active on it.¶

7.3. High-Risk Classes

Four conditions are the high-risk classes the issuance profile defines: irreversible action, external commitment, privileged administration, and a consumption bound ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission]). Where the committed Mission's derived Authority Set or Mission Intent ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-mission-metering]) carries one, the assertion satisfying the accountable-approver rule of Section 6.2 MUST be kind: human, unless a committed, class-named exception under Section 7.1 admits kind: policy for that class.¶

Only governed_policy has a policy accountable assertion (Section 6.2), so a governed policy activates a Mission carrying one of these classes only under such an exception.¶

The same default binds a Mission whose accountable-approver rule is satisfied through its own approval_basis record rather than an assertion (Section 6.2): recording an AGR for such a Mission carrying one of the four classes requires a committed, class-named exception under Section 7.1; absent it, the record MUST NOT be created and, by Section 8, the Mission MUST NOT be created active.¶

7.4. Scope of the Recency Rules

Recency is issuance-time eligibility. It determines whether a Mission activates and does not reach back into a Mission already active: that Mission's governing policy approval was measured fresh at its own approval event, and a later change to the ceiling, the exception, or the policy's freshness does not narrow or terminate it.¶

Approval Governance is an optional extension (Section 1). The rules of Section 7 are a conservative default of this document, not a guarantee of every Mission-Bound Authorization deployment, and a deployment that records no Approval Governance Record is unbounded by them.¶

The kind: human requirement is not by itself an assurance property: the authentication-strength, rendering, and accountable-principal requirements the issuance profile and Section 6 otherwise impose still carry that property; Section 7 only decides which assertion kind MUST be present.¶

7.5. Relationship to Standing-Consent Recency

Section 7 bounds the AGR policy assertion path and, by extension, a standing-consent Mission whose approval_basis record stands in for that assertion (Section 6.2).¶

The issuance profile's standing-consent bases carry their own human-approval instant in approval_basis.approved_at, traced through consent_principal and a commitment ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission]). The template profile's template basis and the progressive profile's ceiling_drawdown basis both populate it, and each profile declares its own maximum age against it: review_cadence for template, and the ceiling review cadence for progressive ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission-template], [I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission-progressive]).¶

Under governed_policy the two instants coincide: approval_basis.approved_at equals the accountable policy assertion's authority.approved_at (Section 11.2). This document's ceiling therefore bounds that basis, alongside any standing-consent recency ceiling the deployment declares ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission], Section "Standing-Consent Recency").¶

The two recency paths are analogous, each bounding staleness against a retained human-approval instant, but they gate different things:¶

  • this document's ceiling governs whether the issuer may count a policy assertion, or activate a Mission whose approval_basis stands in for one, at evaluation; and¶

  • a profile's own maximum age governs whether the dispatch or drawdown may happen at all, independent of whether an AGR is ever recorded.¶

8. Evaluation and Atomic Commitment

Where a deployment records the Approval Governance Record for an approval, the record and the Mission are one commit. Within that commit, the issuer:¶

  • validates the assertions (Section 6);¶

  • evaluates them under the referenced policy version;¶

  • where the record carries approval_context_commitment (Section 5.1), computes it from the Mission Record being created;¶

  • signs the evaluated record (Section 9); and¶

  • persists the signed record in the same commit as the Mission's creation.¶

The only order the commit fixes is evaluate, then sign, then persist, with approval_context_commitment computed before signing; the other checks this document requires, including those of Section 11.3, complete before signing, in whatever order their inputs allow. A value of approval_context_commitment computed after the commit is not covered by the record's signature and MUST NOT be added to a committed record.¶

Where a deployment records the Approval Governance Record for an approval, the Mission Record MUST NOT be created active unless the evaluation's decision is approved and the signed record persists atomically with the Mission's creation. Failure to authenticate the contributing assertions, to complete the evaluation, or to persist the record MUST prevent activation; there is no Mission whose governance record was meant to exist and does not.¶

A governance evaluation that denies creates no Mission and no committed record. Negative assertions retained inside a committed record document dissent (Section 6.1), and a wholly declined approval's evidence surface is consent evidence's declined outcome ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission-consent-evidence]), which exists without a Mission Record.¶

A committed record is immutable. A subsequent governance action, such as a re-review or an incident finding, is a new record about a new event, never an edit.¶

9. Envelope and Verification

The envelope member (Section 5.1) is an object with format (a string, required) and value (a string, required). The default format is jws-compact: a JWS Compact Serialization [RFC7515] over the JCS [RFC8785] canonical bytes of the record with the envelope member removed, signed by the Mission Issuer under a key resolvable in its published key material. The signed record, envelope included, is the retained, registrable form.¶

This document defines only the jws-compact format; an implementation MUST reject an envelope whose format is unsupported rather than accepting it unverified.¶

The JWS protected header MUST carry:¶

  • a kid resolvable in the Mission Issuer's published key material;¶

  • a typ of mission-approval-governance+jws; and¶

  • a cty of mission-approval-governance+json.¶

These values are the registered media types application/mission-approval-governance+jws and application/mission-approval-governance+json (Section 18) with the application/ prefix omitted, as Sections 4.1.9 and 4.1.10 of [RFC7515] recommend.¶

A verifier compares each value as a media type under those sections: it treats a value that contains no / as if application/ were prepended, and it compares type and subtype names case-insensitively. Neither media type defines a parameter, so a value that carries one matches neither. A verifier MUST accept either form of each value, and MUST reject a JWS whose protected header lacks typ or cty, or whose typ or cty does not match this pair. Normalization applies only to the comparison: a verifier MUST NOT rewrite the signed header, the payload, or the retained record, so the record digest below covers the record exactly as signed and retained.¶

The following is the decoded JWS protected header of the envelope value in Section 5.5:¶

{
  "alg": "ES256",
  "kid": "as-key-2",
  "typ": "mission-approval-governance+jws",
  "cty": "mission-approval-governance+json"
}
¶

A verifier MUST perform the following steps, in order, and MUST NOT treat a record as verified if any step fails:¶

  1. Resolve the Mission Issuer's signing key from the JWS protected kid in its published key material.¶

  2. Verify the JWS signature and protected header against that key.¶

  3. Compute the JCS canonical bytes of the outer record with the envelope member removed.¶

  4. Require byte-for-byte equality between the decoded JWS payload and the canonical bytes of step 3, rejecting the record on any difference. The signature authenticates only its own embedded payload; a record that differs from that payload is unauthenticated regardless of whether the signature itself verifies.¶

  5. Validate the mission binding of Section 5.1 against the Mission Record it is retained with.¶

Where the record carries approval_context_commitment, the mission member carries no Mission id, so a verifier recomputes the commitment from the Mission Record this record is retained with, which step 5 resolves, and rejects the reference on a mismatch (Section 14.3).¶

The record digest is the digest, in the encoded form Section 2 fixes, over the JCS [RFC8785] canonical bytes of the complete record, envelope included. It is the one byte sequence every external reference names:¶

  • audit registration hashes exactly these bytes (Section 13); and¶

  • consent evidence's approval_governance_digest equals exactly this value (Section 12).¶

10. Re-checking an Evaluation

A Relying Auditor re-checks an evaluation by replaying it from retained evidence: the policy and any evaluator that decided, the inputs they evaluated, and the recorded assertions. Conformance requires it to obtain the retained policy version before treating the evaluation as re-checked (Section 15). The re-check has these steps:¶

  1. Verify the record (Section 9).¶

  2. Obtain the retained snapshot of the approval_policy version the record names, and recompute approval_policy.digest over it (Section 5.4).¶

  3. Where that snapshot commits a separate evaluator that decides, as a governed_policy root can (Section 11.1), obtain the retained snapshot of that evaluator and recompute its activation policy reference digest ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission], Section "Activation Policy Commitment").¶

  4. Obtain the inputs the deciding policy or evaluator evaluated, as the issuer recorded them ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission], Section "Principals and Approval Basis"). These include each model output in full, with its verdict and any narrowing, which the matching service assertion records only by identifier. Confirm that each input carries this record's approval_event_id, the Mission's intent_hash, and the authority_hash of the set recorded as evaluated, and that each contributing service assertion corresponds to one of them.¶

  5. Re-run the deciding policy or evaluator, at its exact version, over those inputs and the recorded assertions, and confirm that it approves, counting exactly the assertions contributing lists (Section 5.1).¶

  6. Apply the recency rules of Section 7, as the snapshot or the declaration it commits states them, with evaluation.evaluated_at as the evaluation instant.¶

Where a snapshot, the evaluator, or an input cannot be obtained, a recomputed digest does not match, a binding does not hold, or the replay reaches a different outcome, the evaluation is not re-checked.¶

A re-check does not re-authenticate an assertion: the record carries an assertion's authentication context (Section 5.3), not the authentication itself.¶

11. Governed Policy Approval

This document defines the governed_policy standing-consent basis on the issuance profile's extension point for named standing-consent bases ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission], Section "Standing-Consent Bases"). An accountable human approves a bounded policy version once; that policy then activates eligible Mission proposals with no fresh human decision per instance. Every activation under this basis MUST record an Approval Governance Record, committed with the Mission (Section 8).¶

11.1. Consented Root

The consented root is the retained approval_policy snapshot that a human approved (Section 5.4). It commits:¶

  • the clients, Subjects, and authority sources that can activate under it;¶

  • the authority ceiling and the lifetime limits of each activated Mission;¶

  • the deterministic rules that decide an activation;¶

  • the recency ceilings, the clock-skew allowance, and any class-named exceptions of Section 7; and¶

  • where a separate policy artifact decides instances, that artifact's activation policy reference ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission], Section "Activation Policy Commitment").¶

The root decides whether an instance activates. It is distinct from an organizational authority source's authority_source.policy, which bounds whose authority the Mission draws on ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission]).¶

The issuer MUST establish the standing consent, the consenting human, and that human's approval of the exact root version from retained, authenticated governance state, never from the activating request: a submitted proposal cannot supply its own standing consent.¶

The root and a separate evaluator keep distinct digest domains: approval_policy.digest commits the root under typ mission-approval-policy (Section 5.4), and the activation policy reference commits the evaluator under typ mission-activation-policy. The issuer MUST NOT accept either digest in place of the other.¶

11.2. Instance Record

A Mission activated under this basis records these approval_basis members ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission]), each REQUIRED:¶

type:

governed_policy.¶

consent_principal:

The human who approved the exact root version.¶

approved_at:

The instant of that approval, verified against retained, authenticated state as the issuance profile requires of every standing-consent basis.¶

root_commitment:

The root's approval_policy.digest; the record's own approval_policy is that root.¶

activation:

An object of approval_event_id, equal to the Mission's own approval_event_id, and policy, an object of the deciding policy's id, version, and digest.¶

activation_actor:

The authenticated client that requested this activation, distinct from consent_principal.¶

adjudication:

kind policy, with policy naming the deciding policy's id and version, and governance_record true.¶

The deciding policy is the root itself or the separate evaluator the root commits, never another policy. Its digest in activation.policy is approval_policy.digest when the root decides, and the evaluator's mission-activation-policy digest when the evaluator decides.¶

The instance's approval_event_id identifies this activation's approval event, never the human's approval of the root. The issuer allocates one per proposal it activates, so a retried or concurrent activation of the same proposal resolves to one Mission and one record, and each distinct activation creates its own Mission.¶

The record's accountable assertion (Section 6) MUST be a policy assertion whose principal is consent_principal and whose authority names the deciding policy's id and version with approved_at equal to approval_basis.approved_at. Like every contributing assertion, it is authenticated and bound to this approval event. It records the policy's decision under that human's standing authorization, not a fresh human action.¶

11.3. Activation

At the atomic creation commit (Section 8), the issuer MUST, before it creates the Mission active:¶

  1. Resolve the standing consent from retained, authenticated state and confirm that it still admits new activations.¶

  2. Verify that the consenting human was authorized to approve the exact root version and approved it at approved_at, and recompute approval_policy.digest over the retained snapshot.¶

  3. Where the root commits a separate evaluator, recompute that evaluator's digest over the exact snapshot evaluated ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission], Section "Activation Policy Commitment").¶

  4. Establish the Subject and the authority source as the issuance profile requires, and verify that the requesting client, the Subject, and the source are ones the root admits.¶

  5. Derive the Authority Set under the issuance profile's derivation and authority-proposal rules, within the source's ceiling and the root's authority ceiling, and bound the effective expiry by the Mission Intent and the root's lifetime limits.¶

  6. Evaluate the deciding policy, at its exact version, over recorded inputs.¶

  7. Apply the policy-approval recency rules (Section 7) across the whole derived Mission, including any consumption bound, so a high-risk class activates only under a committed, class-named exception.¶

  8. Bind every assertion and the record to this instance's approval_event_id, intent_hash, and authority_hash (Section 6.1, Section 5.1), except that a model output binds to the authority_hash of the set it evaluated.¶

  9. Sign the record and commit it with the active Mission (Section 8).¶

A failure at any step creates no Mission and issues no credential.¶

A model's output enters only as a recorded input at step 6: it can refuse an activation or narrow the authority activated, and never supplies authority or decides the approval ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission], Section "Principals and Approval Basis").¶

Withdrawing the standing consent stops every later activation under it (step 1). A Mission already activated keeps its ordinary lifecycle: withdrawal does not revoke it.¶

14. Approval Context Commitment

This section defines an OPTIONAL profile: one commitment over a Mission's immutable creation facts, so an artifact that authenticates a reference to it can be checked against another authenticated artifact's reference to the same Mission without either re-deriving the other's fields. The manifest is an envelope anchor under the issuance profile's commitment mechanisms, which this document imports normatively ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission]): typ is mission-approval-context-v1, iss is the Mission issuer, and value is the manifest object of Section 14.1. 14.3. Computation and Versioning

An issuer computes approval_context_commitment on demand from the Mission's immutable creation facts, fixed at the approval event ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission]). This document adds no Mission Record member for it; an Approval Governance Record MAY carry it as a member of its own signed record (Section 5), which is a disclosure of the computed value, not a second place the Mission Record holds it. A deployment MAY cache the computed value; a cached value is derived state and MUST NOT be treated as authoritative where it disagrees with a fresh computation from the record.¶

A verifier that recomputes approval_context_commitment from the Mission's immutable creation facts and obtains a value different from the one an artifact discloses MUST reject that artifact's reference as invalid. The recompute always uses this document's fixed typ, mission-approval-context-v1; a value some other party committed under a different typ recomputes to a different digest under this rule and is rejected the same way (Appendix A, vector 6).¶

The member list of Section 14.1 is closed for mission-approval-context-v1. Adding, removing, or redefining a member, or changing how submission_evidence_commitment is constructed, MUST use a new typ, never a silent reinterpretation of this one.¶

14.4. Limits

Reusing the issuance profile's commitment mechanisms supplies canonicalization and algorithm identification and agility. It supplies nothing else:¶

  • No authenticity. The commitment is an unkeyed digest over Mission Record fields held under the same trust as the record itself. An issuer that can rewrite the record can recompute a matching commitment; authentication comes entirely from the signature of whichever artifact discloses the commitment, never from the digest construction.¶

  • No approval-time existence. A matching commitment shows the disclosed facts are internally consistent with the retained record. It does not show the facts were true, or that an approval occurred, at the time claimed.¶

  • No selective disclosure. The commitment is a flat digest over the complete manifest object; proving anything about one member requires disclosing the whole object.¶

14.5. Carriage

approval_context_commitment MAY appear as a signed member of an artifact that already authenticates a reference to the Mission, for example this document's own Approval Governance Record (Section 5) or a Consent Evidence object ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission-consent-evidence]), or under an introspection caller's member-scoped disclosure privilege, the same privilege that already gates proposal_hash ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission]).¶

approval_context_commitment MUST NOT be carried on a Mission-bound access token's baseline mission claim ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission]) or on a credential-bound Mission descriptor a companion profile mints for correlation, for example the Mission Authority Server's Join Assertion ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-mission-authority-server]): each already fixes its own minimal member set, and this commitment is correlation and evidence metadata, never authority.¶

15. Conformance

A Recording Issuer MUST:¶

  • authenticate, event-bind, and policy-authorize every contributing assertion (Section 6.1);¶

  • record denials (Section 6.1);¶

  • satisfy the accountable-approver rule for the Mission's approval basis: exactly one matching human assertion under direct, exactly one matching policy assertion under governed_policy, and the authenticated approval_basis under any other standing-consent basis (Section 6.2);¶

  • evaluate under the retained policy version (Section 8);¶

  • enforce the strictest applicable policy-approval maximum age across the consequence classes the committed Mission's derived Authority Set and Mission Intent carry, from a committed ceiling and any committed exception (Section 7);¶

  • where the committed Mission's derived Authority Set or Mission Intent carries a high-risk class, require a human accountable assertion unless a committed class-named exception applies, and require such an exception for a basis without an accountable assertion (Section 7.3);¶

  • under governed_policy, record the members of Section 11.2 and complete the activation checks of Section 11.3;¶

  • commit the record atomically with Mission creation and let failure prevent activation (Section 8);¶

  • sign the evaluated record before it is committed, and keep the committed record immutable (Section 8, Section 9); and¶

  • retain the record and the referenced policy for the Mission's audit horizon ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission]).¶

A Relying Auditor MUST verify the envelope by byte equality, MUST validate the mission binding, and MUST obtain the retained policy version before treating the evaluation as re-checked (Section 9, Section 5.4, Section 10).¶

16. Security Considerations

The security considerations of the issuance profile ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission]) apply. The following considerations are specific to the Approval Governance Record.¶

16.1. Forged or Padded Assertions

A forged or padded assertion is an authorization attack before commitment, not an audit defect: the assertion requirements of Section 6 are the control, and a deployment that records assertions without authenticating them has built an approval bypass rather than an evidence trail.¶

16.2. Policy Substitution

Without approval_policy.digest and its retention, a later reading cannot prove which policy governed the evaluation. The digest and the audit-horizon retention of Section 5.4 are the control.¶

16.3. Manufactured Accountability

The accountable-approver rule of Section 6.2 prevents a set of service and policy assertions from claiming a Mission whose named Approver never asserted anything. Because that assertion is never service, a model-backed service cannot become the accountable approver: no human-approved policy stands behind its decision, so it contributes only as a governance input.¶

16.4. Stale Policy Approval

Without Section 7, a policy assertion could rely on a human approval far in the past, or stand in for the accountable approver on a high-risk consequence with no human assertion at all. The recency ceiling, its ordering checks, and the human-by-default consequence-class rule are the control.¶

16.5. Immutability Witness

The issuer-signed envelope of Section 9 makes post-commit tampering detectable. Audit registration (Section 13) additionally bounds issuer-side substitution, since a registered record's later alteration diverges from its receipt.¶

The Approval Context Commitment extends this to the Mission Record's creation facts, approval basis included. A verifier that recomputes it and gets a value different from the one an artifact discloses rejects that artifact's reference (Section 14.3). A mismatch shows only that the artifact and the current record disagree. The artifact's signature authenticates the value it discloses, not when it was made, since the issuer can backdate a signed artifact. Where an audit receipt (Section 13) independently shows that the artifact existed before the record changed, and the disclosed value matches a recomputation from the approval event as registered, the mismatch is evidence that the record was altered afterwards.¶

16.6. Issuer Key Compromise

This document inherits the issuance profile's key-material considerations. A compromised Mission Issuer signing key can mint a plausible record; audit registration ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-mission-audit]) is the existence bound, not a substitute for key custody.¶

17. Privacy Considerations

The privacy considerations of the issuance profile ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission]) apply. Assertion principals and decision times are personal data about reviewers. The record is control-plane state retained by the issuer, never disclosed on wire surfaces, and access to a retained record follows the issuer's audit-log discipline for approval-event records ([I-D.draft-mcguinness-oauth-mission]). The approval_context_commitment value is the same in every artifact that discloses it, so it correlates those artifacts (Section 14.5). A service assertion's authority discloses the identifiers and versions of the logic and model that took part in a decision (Section 5.3).¶
